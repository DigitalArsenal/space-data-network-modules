   catch burns tier 1 misses without more false alarms?

## 3. Detectors

Every detector reads the same input: per element set, its trajectory from the
plugged propagator over its two neighbours' epochs on each side (the query
port, section 4), or caller-supplied `$OEM` blocks.

- **D1 (tier 1, unchanged, the baseline).** Crossing time of consecutive
  trajectories; in-track from the vis-viva energy change and cross-track from
  the orbit-normal change at the crossing; median-of-3 step test with k = 8
  robust MAD scale; floors 0.08 m/s (in-track) and 1.0 m/s (cross-track).
- **D2 (level GLR, impulsive).**
  - *Levels.* In-track: each set's orbit-averaged semi-major axis about its own
    epoch (one osculating period, 64 Hermite samples), expressed as in-track
    delta-V, v Δa / (2a). A set's level is taken where its fit is centred, so
    a drag term absorbing a burn cannot leak into it. Cross-track: D1's
    cumulative orbit-normal chain (v Δĥ at each crossing, inertial axes).
  - *Noise.* σ = 1.4826 × the median absolute normalised interpolation
    residual (each level against the line through its neighbours) over ±100
    sets; steps move only two residuals each.
  - *Test.* For each gap between consecutive sets, a robust (Tukey biweight,
    c = 4.685σ) regression on the sets within ±3 days and at most W per side,
    y = α + β (t − τ) + δ H(t − τ): a level, a drag trend and a step.
    Λ = (δ̂ / s_δ)², the generalised likelihood ratio for a jump in a linear
    model (Willsky and Jones 1976). A gap is a change point when Λ ≥ h² and
    |δ̂| ≥ f. Change points are accepted strongest first; each accepted step
    is subtracted before the neighbouring gaps are re-tested, then all steps in
    a window are re-fitted jointly. A second step within one window counts
    only when at least two sets lie between the two change points and their
    levels agree within max(3σ, 25 % of the smaller step) (a single
    partially fitted set after a burn is not a burn).
  - *Time.* The crossing of the last set consistent with the level before the
    step and the first consistent with the level after it, when their spans
    overlap; otherwise the middle of the gap.
  - *Delta-V.* In-track: the step, at the crossing's speed and radius
    (μ Δa / (2 a² v)) when there is one. Cross-track: the step of the normal
    chain, the same test with a vector step, Λ = |δ̂|² / s², against the
    threshold with the same tail probability for three degrees of freedom.
- **D3 (low-thrust).** On D2's in-track levels: a hinge regression within
  ±5 days, y = α + β (t − τ) + γ (t − τ)₊, Λ_γ = (γ̂ / s_γ)². A slope change
  is a low-thrust onset or end when Λ_γ ≥ h_LT², |γ̂| ≥ g (m/s per day), and
  the hinge fits better than D2's step at the same τ. Reported as `$MNV`
  with the along-track acceleration in `MNVR_ACCELS`.
- **C (combined, the candidate default).** D1 ∪ D2: events of the same object
  within 12 h are one event, with D2's time and delta-V when D2 has a
  crossing, otherwise D1's.

## 4. The SDK surface under test

- `detect_maneuvers` takes `$OMM` element sets and asks for their
  trajectories on `propagation_requests` (one query per set: object, epoch,
  span, step, and the caller's `propagator_port_id`). The host routes each
  query to the plugged propagator and replays the request with the answers on
  `propagation` (`$OEM`, one block per query, in order), as the estimation
  module's inverted port does. Caller `$OEM` input stays.
- Providers used: `propagator/sgp4` (all classes, the primary run);
  `propagator/hpop` 1.3.0 full force from `analysis/epoch-state` GCRF states,
  and `propagator/keplerian-reference` (ABI, Earth-fixed output), on two ISS
  months each (Q3; descriptive).
- `compare_ephemerides` (planned-vs-executed and version differences) builds
  the ISS and GNSS truth; `score_detections` matches events to truth. Harness
  JavaScript only selects, reads files and calls modules.

stored, and only aggregates are committed. Space-Track data is never
redistributed.

## 6. Metrics

Per class and detector (D1, D2, C; D3 separately):

- **Recall** with a Wilson 95 % interval. A match: event time in
  [t − 6 h, t + 36 h] of a truth burn at t (ISS, Metop-SG; ISS also at the
  0.2.0 study's [−3 h, +36 h]), [m − 3 d, m + 1 d] (GEO), the truth day ± 1 d
  (GNSS); one-to-one, nearest first.
- **False alarms per object-year:** ISS, events matching no executed burn and
  "unexplained" (NASA issued a plan within the previous day listing no event
  within 1.5 days); Metop-SG, events more than 1.5 days from any MHF burn;
  GNSS, more than 2 days from any truth manoeuvre or gap. GEO and Starlink:
  events per object-year, described, not called false.
- **Burn-time error:** median and 90th percentile of |event − truth|.
- **Delta-V ratio:** detected over truth, median and 10–90 %.
- Recall by delta-V bin: < 0.05, 0.05–0.2, 0.2–1, ≥ 1 m/s.

## 7. Tuning and freeze

Tuned on the ISS tuning window only (the only class with truth there in
numbers); GNSS tuning-window truth is used only to check false alarms.

- D2 grid: W ∈ {4, 6, 8}; h ∈ {5, 6, 7, 8, 10}; f ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.04} m/s;
  cross-track floor ∈ {0.1, 0.25, 0.5} m/s. Choice: the highest recall of C
  with unexplained events no more than D1's on the tuning window; ties go to
  fewer unexplained events, then larger h.
- D3 grid: h_LT ∈ {6, 8, 10}; g ∈ {0.02, 0.05, 0.1} m/s per day. Choice: the
  smallest h_LT and g giving at most 2 D3 events per object-year on the ISS
  tuning window.
- The chosen values are committed to this file (an amendment) and to the
  module's option defaults before the held-out window is read. The held-out
  run uses that commit unchanged.

Established, cited, not claimed: element-set time series and thresholds, GLR
for jumps, change-point segmentation, energy analysis of operator ephemerides,
burn calibration from pre- and post-burn orbits.

Claimed as new (narrow; each holds only if section 8's results do):

1. **A propagator-agnostic detection SDK.** Detection quantities are formed
   between trajectories at one instant, so each propagator's own periodic
   terms cancel, and the trajectories come through an inverted query port from
   whatever propagator is plugged in (SGP4, full-force HPOP, an ABI provider
   with Earth-fixed output), with byte-identical WASM in browser, WasmEdge and
   Docker WasmEdge. No published detector found states its quantities this
   way or swaps propagators behind one interface.
2. **Independent, confirmed truth across five classes on a pre-registered
   held-out split.** ISS burns confirmed by NASA's own later trajectories
   (with the re-issue trap of section 2 closed), Metop-SG's flight-dynamics
   manoeuvre history, precise GNSS orbits, operator post-manoeuvre files and
   operator ephemerides. The works above evaluate on one class, on simulated
   data, or on truth taken from the same element sets.
3. **Two GP failure modes measured and handled:** burns absorbed by a post-burn
   set's drag term (levels about each set's own epoch) and burn pairs hours
   apart (two-step model selection with a minimum intermediate segment).

## 10. Limits stated in advance

- D1's thresholds came from the 2026-09-29 study, which spanned 2021-03 to
  2026-09 and so saw part of the held-out window. D1's held-out numbers are
  not a clean held-out test; D2, D3 and C's are.
- Only the ISS has truth with numbers in the tuning window; other classes test
  transfer.
- GEO truth has one manoeuvre per satellite, with a 3-day time window.
- GNSS truth comes from precise orbits through this module's
  `compare_ephemerides`, a different method on independent data.
- Starlink covers days, not a year; it is descriptive.
- Detection waits for the next element sets (hours for the ISS, a day or more
  for GNSS and GEO). The measurement-level tier (estimation-module innovation
  tests) is not part of this plan.

## Amendments

All made on the tuning window, before the freeze and before any held-out
read.

**A1 (2026-10-11), D2's robust loss.** Huber weights at 1.345σ replace
Tukey's biweight. With Tukey's weights at the levels' own σ (0.010 m/s at the
ISS), a window holding the second burn of the 2024-05-24 pair rejected every
set after the gap, so no step was found at all; Huber's weights never vanish,
and the forward selection finds the second step once the first is accepted.

**A2 (2026-10-11), D2's cross-track levels and floor.** The cross-track chain
is built from each set's mean orbit normal over its own revolution against the
previous set's over the same revolution, not from D1's crossing-time normals
(median per-set change 0.22 m/s against 0.37 m/s, ISS, 2024-04-25 to
2024-06-05). The tails stay heavy (99th percentile 1.4 m/s, maximum 2.5 m/s),
so the pre-registered floor grid {0.1, 0.25, 0.5} m/s would flag noise. The
floor becomes a plane-change angle, v × angle in m/s, so one value serves
every regime: grid {1, 2, 3, 4} × 10⁻⁴ rad (0.77 to 3.1 m/s at the ISS).

**A3 (2026-10-11), D2's time.** The sets on the old and new level are those
within max(3σ, 10 % of the step) of the step fit (sets after a burn settle
with errors that grow with the burn); with 3σ alone, both intermediate sets of
the 2024-05-24 pair were rejected and the time fell back to the gap.

**A4 (2026-10-11), ISS truth.**
- Listings of one burn in different files are one burn when their TIGs are
  within 3 h (a re-planned time); the 0.2.0 study's 6-hour bins are dropped.
- An in-plane plan (no cross-track component, as every NASA plan) is judged by
  the in-track change alone. Across a TIG the mean normal is compared over two
  revolutions, and the nodal-regression correction leaves 0.1 to 0.9 m/s of
  cross-track error at the ISS; judged by the vector, the cancelled 2021-08-19
  and 2022-02-06 burns read as executed or unknown.
- A burn with no clean post-burn version before the next burn is checked with
  it as a group, the group's executed change shared in proportion to the
  plan (the 2024-05-24 pair).
- Tuning-window truth: 58 planned burns, 53 executed, 5 cancelled
  (2021-08-19, 2022-02-06, 2022-06-18, 2022-06-20, 2023-12-21), 0 unknown.

**A5 (2026-10-11), tie-breaks of section 7.** After recall and unexplained
events, ties go to the larger h, then the larger f, then the larger plane
floor, then the smaller W.

**A6 (2026-10-11), the freeze.** ISS tuning window (2020-09-01 to
2024-12-31), 53 executed burns, the 180-point grid of section 7 with A2's
plane floors:
- No grid point met section 7's constraint (unexplained events no more than
